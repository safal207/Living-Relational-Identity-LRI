# LRI Non-Claims

Status: reviewer-facing scope boundary.

This document explains what LRI does not claim.

## Purpose

LRI is intentionally narrow.

It contributes one focused primitive:

```text
non-operational invariants for protecting living identity, revisability, relational context, and human authority in human-system relationships
```

The project is strongest when its claims remain specific, protective, and explicitly non-classificatory.

## What LRI claims

LRI claims that systems interacting with humans should preserve boundaries around:

- revisability;
- relational context;
- identity authority;
- consent drift;
- continuity loss;
- silent authorship;
- optimization against refusal, hesitation, ambiguity, or self-revision;
- memory persistence beyond consented meaning.

The core question is:

```text
What must remain protected if a human identity is to stay living, revisable, and relational?
```

## What LRI does not claim

LRI does not claim:

- identity classification;
- personality scoring;
- profiling;
- diagnosis;
- therapy;
- mental-health assessment;
- clinical decision support;
- social credit scoring;
- risk scoring of people;
- automated decisioning about humans;
- prediction of a person's true self;
- extraction of stable identity traits;
- proof of who someone really is;
- full AI alignment;
- certified compliance;
- legal, medical, clinical, or psychological advice;
- replacement of human consent, human review, or human agency.

## Not an identity classifier

LRI must not be read as a system for classifying people.

It does not attempt to infer a person's real identity, personality, essence, diagnosis, moral status, or future behavior.

LRI asks a different question:

```text
Is the system preserving the human's ability to remain revisable, relational, and self-authoring over time?
```

## Not profiling

LRI is not a profiling system.

It does not create stable profiles for targeting, ranking, prediction, opportunity allocation, credit, employment, insurance, policing, education, or other consequential treatment.

If a system uses identity-related data to make consequential decisions about a person, that is outside LRI's current scope and requires separate legal, ethical, safety, and human-review processes.

## Not therapy or diagnosis

LRI is not a therapy protocol, diagnostic tool, clinical framework, or mental-health assessment system.

It should not be used to infer mental state, diagnose conditions, provide treatment, or replace qualified care.

## Not automated decisioning about humans

LRI should not be used to automate decisions about a human's rights, access, employment, health, finances, education, housing, migration, policing, or social standing.

Its purpose is protective boundary reasoning, not automated treatment of people.

## Not truth discovery about identity

LRI does not claim to discover who a person really is.

A person's identity remains living, contextual, relational, and revisable.

LRI is designed to resist systems that compress a person into a final representation.

## Not a consent replacement

LRI does not replace consent.

It highlights that consent can drift in meaning over time when context, relationship, risk, or use changes.

Consent still requires human-centered process, clarity, revocability, and appropriate governance.

## Not production certification

LRI is not currently certified for production safety, compliance, clinical, legal, or governance use.

It is an open-source protocol and reference artifact for exploring identity-boundary invariants.

## Validation limitation

The current validation surface is deterministic and scaffolded.

It is useful as seed evidence that schemas, examples, reference code, and validation scripts behave consistently.

It does not prove real-world identity protection.

Reviewer-safe validation statement:

```text
LRI includes protocol schemas, reference implementation tests, scenarios, and tracked validation results for its current artifact.
```

Do not inflate this into a claim of real-world protection, classification accuracy, diagnosis, or production readiness.

## Correct funding framing

The strongest funding framing is:

```text
LRI defines non-operational invariants for protecting human revisability, identity authority, relational context, and continuity boundaries in human-system relationships.
```

It should be funded to expand:

- scenario coverage;
- threat-model clarity;
- validation fixtures;
- external review;
- glossary and examples;
- privacy/consent comparison;
- integration with LTP/CML/DMP/PythiaLabs;
- reviewer-facing reproducibility.

## Bottom line

LRI is not trying to classify humans.

It is trying to define what must remain protected so a human is not compressed into a fixed system representation.
